Foundation — Section 1

What Is AI Governance?

Before touching any software, understand the concept. AI Governance is the answer to a very specific business problem that every organisation using AI faces today.

The Problem in Plain Terms

Imagine your company uses AI in 10 different places — a chatbot, a fraud detector, a hiring tool, a loan approval system, a medical diagnosis aid. Each one makes decisions that affect real people. Now ask yourself these questions:

  • Can you tell a regulator exactly what AI systems you run and what they do?
  • If one of them discriminates against a minority group, do you know about it?
  • If your AI makes a wrong decision and someone is harmed — who is responsible?
  • Can you prove, with documents and evidence, that you assessed the risks before deploying?
  • Is there a written policy governing how your team builds and uses AI?
  • When something goes wrong, do you have a process to report it to regulators?

The harsh reality: Most organisations today cannot answer YES to most of these questions. They use AI extensively but have zero documented governance. Under the EU AI Act, that is now a legal violation with fines up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover.

What AI Governance Actually Is

AI Governance is the set of processes, policies, controls, and evidence that prove your organisation is using AI responsibly. Think of it like financial accounting — just as you track every dollar and prove to auditors that your finances are sound, AI governance tracks every AI system and proves to regulators that your AI is safe, fair, and compliant.

Foundation — Section 2

Why Parkar GovernAI Exists

The problem is not new — but the urgency is. Three things converged to make AI governance a business-critical priority right now.

1. Regulation Has Arrived

The EU AI Act came into force in August 2024 — the world's first comprehensive AI law. It applies to ANY organisation that deploys AI systems affecting EU citizens, regardless of where the organisation is based. Penalties are severe and enforcement is already beginning.

2. AI Is Everywhere and Out of Control

The average enterprise runs hundreds of AI systems — many deployed by individual teams without central oversight. Nobody knows the full inventory. Nobody tracks the risks. Nobody has the evidence. This is the shadow AI problem.

3. Spreadsheets Don't Work at Scale

The traditional approach is spreadsheets — one for risk, one for models, one for policies. They get outdated, they're not connected, they produce no evidence trail, and they fail every audit. Parkar GovernAI replaces the entire spreadsheet ecosystem with a single, connected platform.

What Parkar GovernAI solves: It gives organisations a single system of record for every AI governance activity — from registering an AI system to generating a regulator-ready compliance report — eliminating spreadsheets, email chains, and the risk of non-compliance.

Foundation — Section 3

Regulatory Frameworks Explained Simply

You don't need to be a lawyer to understand these. Here is what each framework actually means in plain English — and how Parkar GovernAI maps to each one.

🇪🇺 EU Law
EU AI Act
The world's first binding AI law. Classifies AI systems by risk level. High-risk systems (loans, hiring, medical) must be documented, risk-assessed, and transparent. Serious incidents must be reported to regulators.
🌐 ISO Standard
ISO 42001
The international standard for AI Management Systems. Like ISO 9001 for quality — it tells you HOW to build an AI governance program. 38 clauses + 93 annex controls covering everything from leadership commitment to incident management.
🌐 ISO Standard
ISO 27001
The international standard for Information Security Management. AI systems process sensitive data — ISO 27001 ensures that data is protected. Often required alongside ISO 42001 for complete AI governance.
🇺🇸 US Framework
NIST AI RMF
The US National Institute of Standards voluntary AI Risk Management Framework. 4 functions: Govern (set up the program), Map (identify AI risks), Measure (analyse them), Manage (treat and monitor). Widely adopted globally.

Risk Management

The central AI risk register. Import from IBM/MIT databases, score, assign, and track mitigation.

/risk-management

Most organisations start AI risk assessment with a blank spreadsheet. Parkar GovernAI gives you two curated databases — IBM (113 risks) and MIT (academic research) — so your risk register is populated in minutes, not weeks.

Risk Level Calculation Formula

Risk Level = (Likelihood × 1) + (Severity × 3)
Severity is weighted 3× because a catastrophic but rare event must still be prioritised over a frequent low-severity one. This mirrors EU AI Act risk methodology.

The 4-Section Incident Form

  • Section 1 — Incident Information: AI project, type (7 options: Malfunction, Unexpected behavior, Model drift, Misuse, Data corruption, Security breach, Performance degradation), severity, status, occurred date, detected date, reporter name, model version
  • Section 2 — Impact Assessment: Harm categories (Health, Safety, Fundamental Rights, Property, Environment, Financial, Reputation, Fairness), affected persons, description, causality analysis
  • Section 3 — Response: Immediate mitigations taken, planned corrective actions
  • Section 4 — Approval: Approval status, approver, notes, interim report toggle for ongoing multi-phase incidents

Glossary of Key Terms

Plain-English definitions for every term you'll encounter during the demo.

TermPlain English Definition
Use CaseOne AI system registered in the platform (e.g., "Loan Approval AI"). The central governance object — everything else links to it.
EU AI ActThe world's first binding AI law (EU, 2024). Classifies AI by risk level. High-risk systems need full documentation. Fines up to €35M.
ISO 42001International standard for AI Management Systems. Certification you can earn to prove your AI governance program is structured correctly.
NIST AI RMFUS voluntary framework for AI risk management. 4 functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Widely adopted globally.
High Risk AIEU AI Act category for AI that affects people significantly (loans, hiring, medical, biometrics). Requires full compliance documentation.
Compliance ControlA specific requirement from a framework (e.g., ISO 42001 Clause 8.2 = "Conduct AI risk assessment"). You assign an owner and track status.
Risk RegisterThe list of all identified risks — what could go wrong, how likely, how severe, who is responsible, and what is being done.
EvidenceA document that proves you did what you claim (e.g., a Security Assessment Report proves you assessed the model's security).
Model CardA standard document describing an AI model — what it does, its limitations, known biases, training data. Required by EU AI Act Article 13.
MitigationActions taken to reduce a risk. A bias risk might be mitigated by removing demographic identifiers from training data.
Organisational ProjectThe single master project in the Frameworks module that holds all ISO/NIST controls for the whole organisation. One-time setup required.
Vendor ScorecardAuto-calculated 0–100 risk score for a third-party AI vendor based on data sensitivity, business criticality, past incidents, and regulatory exposure.
INC-XXXXAuto-generated incident ID (e.g., INC-0001). Immutable reference used in EU AI Act Article 73 regulatory reports.
FRIAFundamental Rights Impact Assessment. Required by EU AI Act Article 27 for High-Risk AI deployed by public authorities.
Shadow AIAI systems running in an organisation that the governance team doesn't know about. Agent Discovery finds and inventories these.
AI Trust CenterPublic-facing webpage showing your AI governance posture, compliance badges, and published policies. No login required for visitors.
Residual RiskThe risk level that remains AFTER mitigation actions have been taken. Lower than the initial risk level if mitigation is effective.
Post-Market MonitoringOngoing surveillance of a deployed AI system to detect performance degradation, new risks, or regulatory changes. EU AI Act Article 72.
