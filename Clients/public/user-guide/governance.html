Frameworks

The compliance control center — map every governance activity against ISO 42001, ISO 27001, and NIST AI RMF. Assign owners, track status, and attach evidence per control.

/framework

The Frameworks page is where compliance work happens. ISO 42001 has 38 clauses and 93 annex controls. NIST AI RMF has 106 subcategories. Managing these in spreadsheets is error-prone and not audit-ready. Here, every control has an owner, a status, a due date, and attached evidence — in one place.

Page Tabs

  • Dashboard: Framework completion donuts, assignment status %, control status breakdown, per-category progress bars.
  • Framework Risks: Organizational-level risks linked to specific controls.
  • Linked Models: All models from Model Inventory mapped to this framework's requirements.
  • Requirements and Controls (core work area): Every clause, annex, and subcategory with inline editing of status, owner, reviewer, due date, and evidence.
  • Settings: Enable or disable frameworks for the organization.

Use Cases

The central AI system registry. Every AI deployment in the organization gets a Use Case — the governance container that holds all compliance controls, risks, models, and evidence for that system.

/home

A Use Case (also called a "Project") is the most fundamental object in Parkar GovernAI. Everything else — risks, models, datasets, compliance controls, vendors — links back to a Use Case. EU AI Act requires organizations to enumerate all AI systems, classify them by risk level, and demonstrate compliance per system. This is that registry.

Creating a Use Case

Key fields when registering a new AI system:

FieldDescription
UC-IDAuto-generated (UC-1, UC-2…) — immutable reference number for regulatory submissions
AI Risk ClassificationProhibited / High risk / Limited risk / Minimal risk (EU AI Act Article 6)
RoleDeployer / Provider / Distributor / Importer / Manufacturer / Authorized Rep
Linked FrameworksEU AI Act, ISO 42001, ISO 27001, NIST AI RMF — multi-select
Owner + MembersTeam assignment with granular roles
GeographyJurisdiction context for regulatory applicability
StatusNot started → In progress → Under review → Completed / Rejected / On hold

Inside a Use Case — 5 Tabs

  • Overview: Compliance progress per framework, risk count by severity, linked members, vendors, models, and datasets.
  • Risks: All risks scoped to this specific AI system — a filtered view of the global risk register.
  • Project Settings: Edit framework assignments, member roles, classification, FRIA (EU AI Act Article 27), and CE Marking documentation.
  • Activity: Full immutable change history — every field change, status transition, and member addition with timestamps.
  • Post-Market Monitoring: Ongoing monitoring questions for deployed AI systems (EU AI Act Article 72).

FRIA: The Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment, required by EU AI Act Article 27 for High-Risk AI systems deployed by public authorities. The FRIA section is accessible within each use case's Project Settings tab.

PII Tracking

When Contains PII is marked as Yes, you must also specify PII types (names, health data, financial data, location data, etc.). This automatically:

  • Triggers GDPR/CCPA compliance obligations for that dataset
  • Flags the dataset in the Dashboard as requiring data protection review
  • Requires bias mitigation documentation before the use case can be marked compliant

Use Case → Model → Dataset
A regulator can follow this chain to see: which AI system uses which model, which model was trained on which data, and what governance was applied to that data.

Agent Discovery

Automatically discover every AI agent running in your environment. Eliminate shadow AI — you cannot govern what you do not know about.

/agent-discovery

Most enterprises don't know all the AI agents running in their environment — GitHub Copilot agents, Azure AI Foundry agents, custom LLM pipelines, Slack bots. EU AI Act Article 14 requires human oversight of AI systems. You cannot have oversight of agents you've never seen. Agent Discovery solves this.

Stale Agent Detection

Agents with no activity for 30+ days are automatically flagged as Stale. This enables cleanup of abandoned AI deployments and helps with EU AI Act documentation accuracy.

Important: Stale agents are not automatically deactivated or deleted — they are flagged for your review. The governance team decides whether to confirm, reject, or unlink each stale agent.

Risk Management

The central AI risk register. Identify, import, assess, and track risks across all AI systems — with curated databases from IBM and MIT.

/risk-management

Most organizations start their AI risk program with a blank spreadsheet. Parkar GovernAI gives you two curated, research-backed databases to import from — so your risk register is populated in minutes, not weeks.

Risk Level Calculation

Risk Level = (Likelihood × 1) + (Severity × 3)
Severity is weighted 3× — a catastrophic but unlikely event still warrants high attention. This mirrors EU AI Act risk assessment methodology.

Three Ways to Add Risks

  • Manual entry — Full form with all fields: name, description, AI lifecycle phase, likelihood, severity, mitigation plan, framework linkages, and approval workflow.
  • IBM AI Risk Database (Recommended) — 113 curated risks covering agentic AI, data privacy violations, inference attacks, and operational failures. Browse, select, review, and save.
  • MIT AI Risk Repository — Academic research-based risks covering AI safety, fairness, accountability, transparency, and societal impact.

AI Lifecycle Phases

Each risk is tagged to the phase where it manifests:

  1. Problem definition
  2. Data collection and preparation
  3. Model development
  4. Model evaluation and testing
  5. Deployment
  6. Monitoring and maintenance
  7. Decommissioning

Training Registry

Track AI literacy and governance training programs across the organization — the auditable record needed to satisfy EU AI Act Article 4.

/training-registry

EU AI Act Article 4 requires organizations to take measures to ensure sufficient AI literacy. ISO 42001 requires documented training programs. The Training Registry is your auditable evidence that people across the organization have been trained.

Two Tabs

  • Trainings: All training programs with name, duration, provider, department, status, and participant count.
  • Evidence Hub (Training scope): Completion certificates, attendance records, training materials, and assessment results linked to training programs.

Training Statuses

StatusProgressRules
Planned0%Scheduled, not yet started. Participant count can be edited.
In Progress50%Currently running. Participant count cannot be reduced.
Completed100%Finished. Status cannot revert to Planned. Count locked.

Audit trail protection: Once a training is Completed, you cannot reduce the participant count or revert the status. This ensures the record serves as a reliable audit trail rather than editable retrospective data.

Department-Level Coverage Reporting

By adding a Department field to each training, you get department-level coverage metrics visible on the Dashboard:

  • "Legal team: 100% trained in EU AI Act fundamentals"
  • "Engineering team: 60% trained in AI Safety"
  • "Management: 100% completed AI Ethics awareness"

Certificate Expiry Tracking

Evidence documents in the Training Evidence Hub have an expiry date. When a certification approaches its expiry, it appears in the Dashboard's "Files needing attention" section — so you never miss a renewal.

Reporting

Generate board-ready, regulator-ready compliance reports in PDF or DOCX — fully branded with your organization's logo and colors — in under 30 seconds.

/reporting

The Reporting module converts all governance data — risks, compliance controls, assessments, vendors, models, training, policies — into a professionally formatted document. What would take a consultant days to compile is generated in seconds.

Report Scopes

  • Use Case Report: All governance data for one specific AI system (e.g., "Fraud Detection AI"). Use when a regulator asks about a specific deployment.
  • Organization Report: Cross-all-use-cases comprehensive view of the entire AI governance posture. Use for board reporting and annual governance reviews.

Generating a Report — Step by Step

1
Choose scopeUse Case (specific system) or Organization (all systems).
2
Select frameworkEU AI Act, ISO 42001, ISO 27001, or NIST AI RMF.
3
Choose sectionsRisk Analysis (Use Case Risks, Vendor Risks, Model Risks), Compliance (Controls, Assessment Tracker, Clauses), Organization (Models, Vendors, Training, Policies, Incidents).
4
Apply brandingUpload your logo, set primary and secondary colors, enter your organization name.
5
Choose format and generatePDF (non-editable, for regulators) or DOCX (editable, for internal stakeholders). Report generates in under 30 seconds.

What the Report Contains

  • Branded cover page with your logo, organization name, framework, and report date
  • Table of contents
  • Executive summary with governance score and key metrics
  • Risk distribution charts (SVG-embedded horizontal bars and donuts)
  • Framework control status tables
  • Compliance progress per section
  • Evidence references

Format guidance: Use PDF for regulatory submissions and external auditors — it is non-editable and preserves formatting exactly. Use DOCX for board presentations and internal stakeholders who need to add annotations or comments.

If your organization uses third-party AI tools — OpenAI APIs, AWS Rekognition, Azure AI services, HuggingFace models — you have vendor governance obligations. EU AI Act Article 28 imposes specific requirements on deployers who use third-party AI systems. This module provides the documented evidence trail.

When you create or update a vendor record, the scorecard automatically calculates a composite risk score based on four dimensions:

DimensionOptions

Review Statuses

Not Started In Review Reviewed Requires Follow-up

Vendor Risks Tab

Each vendor can have multiple associated risks in the Vendor Risks sub-register. Key fields:

  • Risk description — what the risk is (e.g., "Data residency: EU customer data processed outside EU")
  • Impact and likelihood — from Negligible to Catastrophic
  • Action plan — mitigation strategy
  • Action owner — the person responsible for executing the plan
  • Use case links — which AI systems are affected by this vendor risk

Example: If you use OpenAI's API and your users' data is sent to OpenAI's servers, create a vendor record for OpenAI with Data Sensitivity = PII, Business Criticality = High, and Regulatory Exposure = EU AI Act + GDPR. The scorecard will calculate a high risk score, triggering a mandatory review.

Incident Management

EU AI Act Article 73 serious incident reporting — structured capture, harm category mapping, approval workflow, and CE Marking integration for complete regulatory compliance.

/ai-incident-management

EU AI Act Article 73 requires providers and deployers of High-Risk AI systems to report serious incidents to market surveillance authorities. Failure to report is a regulatory violation. This module provides the structured workflow for capturing, investigating, and reporting every AI incident.

Incident Lifecycle

Open → Investigating → Mitigated → Closed

7 Incident Types

Malfunction Unexpected behavior Model drift Misuse Data corruption Security breach Performance degradation

3 Severity Levels

SeverityDescriptionReporting Obligation
MinorLimited impact, no significant harmInternal documentation only
SeriousSignificant impact, potential harmRegulatory reporting may be required
Very SeriousMajor harm to people or societyEU AI Act Article 73 reporting obligation triggered

Categories of Harm (EU AI Act Aligned)

At least one harm category must be selected for every incident:

  • Health
  • Safety
  • Fundamental Rights
  • Property
  • Environment
  • Financial Impact
  • Customer Trust / Reputation
  • Fairness / Ethical Concerns

The 4-Section Incident Form

  • Section 1 — Incident Information: AI project, type, severity, status, occurred date, date detected, reporter, model version. Note: date_detected must be ≥ occurred_date.
  • Section 2 — Impact Assessment: Harm categories, affected persons/groups, description (5–1000 chars), relationship/causality analysis.
  • Section 3 — Response and Actions: Immediate mitigations already taken, planned corrective actions.
  • Section 4 — Approval and Reporting: Approval status, approver, approval notes, and the Interim Report toggle for ongoing incidents requiring periodic updates to regulators.

Auto-Generated Incident IDs

Every incident receives an auto-generated ID (e.g., INC-0047). These IDs are immutable and provide unambiguous incident references for regulatory submissions, cross-referencing with change history, and CE Marking documentation.

CE Marking Integration: Incidents can be linked directly to CE Marking conformity records — connecting incident reporting to regulatory filing in one system. This satisfies the documentation chain required for High-Risk AI system conformity declarations.
